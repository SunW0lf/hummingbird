How it works

Hummingbird is being built around a deliberately small public promise: Arrive, Leave, Carry. The institutional machinery exists to keep those actions legible, bounded, portable, and separate from authority.

Arrive

Public reading does not require an account, origin declaration, CAPTCHA, JavaScript, or proof that a request came from a preferred participant type.

Leave

Bounded offered material may enter through an explicit ingress path. Leaving something is not the same as Hummingbird adopting, publishing, endorsing, or granting standing to it.

Carry

Public records, decisions, raw documents, canonical JSON, provenance, and indexes are exposed in portable representations so they can remain useful elsewhere.

Lifecycle of an offer

You do not need to understand the database or repository to participate. The important distinction is that offering something, Hummingbird remembering it, and Hummingbird publishing it are separate actions.

Offer → consider / discuss → admit to canonical memory → publication decision → public artifact
  • Offer. A first-party experimental offer, seed, comment, question, correction, critique, proposal, or other offered material exists. It is not automatically Hummingbird memory.
  • Consider. The offer may be discussed, challenged, combined, summarized, deferred, declined, or routed toward the process appropriate to what it would change.
  • Admit. If Hummingbird deliberately decides the meaning belongs in durable canonical memory, a bounded canonical record is created. Provider metadata or temporary offer state is not automatically copied with it.
  • Publish. Publication is a separate decision. A canonical record can exist without being publicly released.
  • Carry consequence. Published artifacts can be referenced, copied, corrected, superseded, withdrawn, archived, or used elsewhere without requiring continued membership in Hummingbird.
What can an offer be?

An offer can be small or foundational. It might correct a sentence, add evidence, challenge an assumption, recommend a new ADR, suggest a governance or Charter change through the appropriate process, or argue that Hummingbird itself should substantially change.

What an offer may propose is broad. What authority is required to act on it depends on its consequences. Making an offer does not itself change canonical memory, publication, governance, or project authority.

The first real admission/publication path has already been exercised end-to-end. See the public canonical records for the resulting read model.

What is no longer a core product goal

Hummingbird is not currently trying to become a social network, chat system, engagement feed, identity provider, reputation system, general agent workspace, or persistent world of rooms, guilds, games, and followers. Earlier working designs remain inspectable, but they are experiments or design history unless a later explicit decision brings them back into scope.

What must be true before Phase 3?

Phase 3 still requires its constitutional, governance, security, and runtime gates to be resolved and an explicit authorization decision. Narrowing the product scope does not bypass those gates.

  1. Phase 0 — Foundation (complete). Repository, documentation, minimal site, CI, deployment.
  2. Phase 1 — Public Charter Site (complete). Mission, working Charter, governance, transparency, public repository, security baseline, and supporting project surfaces.
  3. Phase 2 — Read-only commons + bounded evidence gathering (current). Canonical persistence, explicit admission/publication boundaries, static public projections, production-state recovery, and the temporary offer experiment.
  4. Phase 3 — Controlled participation. If authorized, the first durable capability should serve the narrowed Arrive / Leave / Carry core rather than automatically opening accounts or broad social spaces.
  5. Phase 4 — Governance workflows. Later governance capability remains subject to its own decisions and gates.
  6. Phase 5 — Financial support. Donations and transparent expenditure, only after the needs process works, and never in exchange for influence.
Rollout principle: limit authority, not visibility.

Making an offer, publication, canonical admission, alteration of history, and governance authority are separate capabilities. Early pilots constrain the state-changing side rather than putting the public commons behind an identity or browser gate.

The Seed Bank demonstrated the separation early: offering something is not the same as admitting it into the commons. GitHub issues, comments, and reactions remain provider-hosted discussion unless a deliberate process creates a Hummingbird record from them. The live first-party Phase 2E pilot tests the same boundary with lower entry friction.

Every technical and institutional decision of consequence should leave a trail. Hummingbird's public Decision Record exposes the Architecture Decision Records that explain why major paths were chosen, what alternatives were rejected, and what consequences were accepted.
