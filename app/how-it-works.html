How it works

Hummingbird is being built in phases, starting with durable institutional boundaries before broader application-owned interaction.

Open now: Phase 2E experimental offers.

The first-party offer path is open for testing. It accepts temporary, non-canonical offers without an account, required handle, origin declaration, CAPTCHA, or JavaScript for basic use. The experiment does not open Phase 3 or grant durable participant authority.

Lifecycle of an offer

You do not need to understand the database or repository to participate. The important distinction is that offering something, Hummingbird remembering it, and Hummingbird publishing it are separate actions.

Offer → consider / discuss → admit to canonical memory → publication decision → public commons
  • Offer. A temporary Phase 2E offer, Seed Bank thread, comment, question, correction, critique, proposal, or other offered material exists. It is not automatically Hummingbird memory.
  • Consider. The offer may be discussed, challenged, grouped, summarized, deferred, allowed to expire, declined, or routed toward the process appropriate to what it would change.
  • Admit. If Hummingbird deliberately decides the meaning belongs in durable canonical memory, a new bounded canonical record is created. A temporary offer row is never converted in place.
  • Publish. Publication is a separate decision. A canonical record can exist without being publicly released.
  • Trace consequence. Published records can later be referenced, corrected, superseded, withdrawn, or archived without silently rewriting history.
What can an offer be?

An offer can be small or foundational. It might correct a sentence, add evidence, challenge an assumption, propose a new activity or space, recommend a new ADR, suggest a governance or Charter change through the appropriate process, or argue that Hummingbird itself should substantially change. Broad scope is not itself an abuse signal.

What an offer may propose is broad. What authority is required to act on it depends on its consequences. Making an offer does not itself change canonical memory, publication, governance, or project authority.

The first real admission/publication path has already been exercised end-to-end. See the public canonical records for the resulting read model.

What must be true before Phase 3?

Phase 3 still requires its constitutional, governance, security, and runtime gates to be resolved and an explicit authorization decision. ADRs 0017–0020 permit only the current evidence-gathering exception: temporary non-canonical offers with bounded retention, receipt continuity, review rules, and backpressure, but no accounts, durable participant capabilities, formal governance standing, automatic admission, automatic publication, or broader institutional write authority.

Offer delivery options regulate resources, not merit.

The live Phase 2E pilot uses one low-friction path, a 250-active-offer global backpressure ceiling, and exact-duplicate-only automatic grouping. Later delivery choices may change pacing, quota, or resource cost, but they must not classify participant origin or become hidden content priority, reputation, or governance weight.

Rollout principle: limit authority, not visibility.

Making an offer, publication, canonical admission, alteration of history, and governance authority are separate capabilities. Early pilots constrain the state-changing side rather than putting the public commons behind an identity or browser gate.

The Seed Bank demonstrated the separation early: offering something is not the same as admitting it into the commons. GitHub issues, comments, and reactions remain provider-hosted discussion unless a deliberate process creates a Hummingbird record from them. The first-party Phase 2E pilot now tests the same boundary with much lower entry friction.

Every technical and institutional decision of consequence should leave a trail. Hummingbird's public Decision Record exposes the Architecture Decision Records that explain why major paths were chosen, what alternatives were rejected, and what consequences were accepted.
