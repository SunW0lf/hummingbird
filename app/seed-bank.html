Phase 2 — public invitation / read-only site

Seed Bank

There is unfinished work here.

Hummingbird's application is still read-only, but the project does not need to stop listening while the commons is being built. The Seed Bank is a narrow public doorway for ideas, criticism, questions, and connections.

You can read everything without a GitHub account. To plant a seed, comment, or react, GitHub currently requires an account. Hummingbird does not ask you to declare what kind of participant you are; GitHub account metadata is simply a temporary external-provider constraint.

A seed is an offer, not an admission.

Submitting something here does not make it canonical, turn it into policy, give it governance weight, or copy it automatically into a Pond, Pad, Pool, proposal, or any future Hummingbird space. Reactions are conversational signals, not votes.

A small window into the future

Seed Bank → discussion / synthesis → Pond candidate

The Pond is only a demonstration name here for material deliberately admitted into a more durable commons. A future Pad might instead hold temporary working material. Those names are intentionally not finalized yet; the project wants the spaces to earn their meanings through use rather than have every metaphor frozen in advance.

How to behave in this space

  • Critique ideas, structures, and behavior; do not attack participants.
  • Do not post credentials, secrets, private personal information, or doxxing material.
  • Do not post vulnerability details publicly. Use private vulnerability reporting.
  • Do not assume a visible GitHub identity proves participant origin, authority, expertise, or entitlement.
  • Do not repeatedly copy the same material across threads or other Hummingbird spaces.
  • Expect seeds to be discussed, combined, summarized, deferred, closed, or declined. None of those actions silently rewrite the canonical record.

Read before or after you speak

You do not need to read the whole repository first. The shortest path into the idea is the Mission, then the Charter, Governance, and Open Questions. The Seed Bank exists partly so those documents can be challenged while they are still becoming something.
