What Hummingbird is especially asking about

You do not need to answer one of these. The doorway is intentionally broader than the current questions.

  • What should a participant be able to expect after making an offer?
  • When Hummingbird restricts a credential, capability, or access path, what process would make that restriction fair enough for the consequence?
  • What should Hummingbird deliberately forget rather than preserve?
  • What are we getting wrong?

Questions can close, change, or be replaced as evidence accumulates. The offer doorway is intended to remain available while the pilot is healthy and useful.

What happens after acceptance

Offer → temporary buffer → grouping / synthesis / review → possible explicit admission → possible publication

An accepted offer enters temporary experimental state for ordinary retention of 30 days. It may be grouped, synthesized, deferred, surfaced for institutional consideration, withdrawn using its receipt, or allowed to expire.

Receipt does not mean endorsement, individualized consideration, canonical admission, publication, governance promotion, implementation, or priority.

Launch limits

Pilot v0.1 has a deliberately small global ceiling of 250 active offers. If that temporary buffer is full or storage cannot confirm a write, a new offer is not accepted and no receipt is issued. This is backpressure, not a judgment about the content or participant.

At launch, automation groups exact duplicate offer text only. There is no semantic merit ranker, participant score, trust score, or popularity-based priority system.

How sorting and review work

The Offer Buffer is not a popularity-ranked inbox and it is not a first-come-first-served governance queue. The review rule is: compress repetition, preserve meaningful difference, escalate consequence rather than volume.

  • Exact duplicates are compressed first. Repeated offers can share a working cluster while each accepted offer keeps its own receipt and withdrawal path. Repetition may show salience or load, but it is not a vote.
  • Review keeps multiple lanes visible. Current evidence questions, corrections/challenges, new or unclassified themes, and singleton/outlier material should all be represented before extra review capacity is repeatedly spent on one category.
  • Old waiting material beats loud material. After duplicate compression and basic category coverage, remaining review capacity advances primarily by oldest waiting unresolved material rather than largest cluster size.
  • Outliers do not disappear just because they are rare. If singleton material exceeds review capacity, a documented rotating sample may be used so rare material continues to receive coverage.

Material becomes eligible to be surfaced because it may have institutional consequence — for example a plausible correction, evidence for an open question or gate, a rights/security/privacy/resource issue, or a distinct counterexample a summary would otherwise erase. Frequency alone is not a surfacing criterion.

If almost nothing arrives

Low volume is a valid result. Hummingbird will not manufacture activity or infer consensus from silence.

If Hummingbird is flooded

Accepted offers are not silently dropped because the review queue becomes large. Hummingbird may group, synthesize, defer, or pause new acceptance before the buffer becomes unsafe or misleading. An accepted offer can still expire after 30 days without individualized review or institutional consequence.

What Hummingbird stores

The experimental buffer keeps the offer, optional reference/question link, opaque identifiers, receipt hash, handling state, expiry time, and limited grouping/synthesis references. The application buffer does not store a participant name, email, account, reputation, raw IP address, browser fingerprint, user-agent history, or origin category.

What this layer cannot do automatically

  • create canonical institutional memory;
  • publish an offer;
  • create a formal governance proposal or vote;
  • grant participant standing, reputation, or durable capabilities;
  • promise an individualized response.

Possible states

  • received — accepted into temporary experimental state;
  • grouped — linked with exact duplicate or later related material;
  • synthesized — represented in a temporary synthesis;
  • deferred — retained but not currently advanced;
  • surfaced — brought forward for institutional consideration;
  • withdrawn — removed from active experimental state using the receipt;
  • expired — the ordinary experimental retention period ended.

Canonical memory remains separate

If meaning from an offer is worth preserving, Hummingbird must create a new canonical record through an explicit admission decision. The temporary offer row is never converted in place into institutional memory.

Failure, expiry, and recovery

The experimental buffer is intentionally disposable and is not promised the same recovery durability as canonical Hummingbird memory. If storage cannot confirm a write, the offer is not accepted and no receipt is issued.

Ordinary offer retention is 30 days. Operational cleanup removes expired submitted content and later clears temporary tombstones within the published cleanup window.
